Project FlyBy · Phase P3 · November 2026

Reminders for people who do not use Gmail

Alex sends from Apple Mail with a Yahoo account (often from an iPhone). Sam uses AOL, maybe through Outlook; we still have to ask. Do we need an IMAP server for them? Probably not as the first step. This page shows why, and the steps from here.

What any solution must do

Gmail users get all three for free, because FlyBy runs inside their own mailbox. For someone on Yahoo or AOL, each one needs another answer.

1 · Watch

Know which mails to watch

  • Gmail: a button, or a Bcc code like me+3d.
  • Everyone else: the same Bcc trick works in any mail app.

2 · Notice

Know if somebody answered

  • Needs a look into the mailbox that received the answer.
  • Gmail: FlyBy already has it. Yahoo / AOL: only IMAP (or an app on the Mac) can.

3 · Return

Bring it back where they look

  • Cheapest: a normal email that lands in their inbox.
  • Fancier: IMAP can copy the original mail into the inbox as unread.

Option A · a reminder by Bcc email (no mailbox access)

Alex adds one Bcc address that carries the time. That copy reaches a FlyBy Gmail mailbox, the engine we already have waits for the date, and sends Alex an ordinary email. Nothing to install and no password.

Good

Works for everybody

  • AOL on the web, the iPhone Mail app, Outlook, anything that can Bcc.
  • No app password, no IMAP, no VPS; reuses the engine and the 5-minute check.

Weak

Blind to replies, and a privacy cost

  • It cannot tell that someone answered; Alex’s one tap on the link does that.
  • The FlyBy mailbox receives a copy of every mail they Bcc. It would keep only subject, recipients and date, and delete the rest.
  • Only allowed senders get reminders, since anyone can forge a From line.

Needs your OK

A small change to a rule

  • AGENTS.md says each person runs their own copy. Here two friends would use one mailbox of yours.
  • No Google check is needed, since only you grant Google access. It is still a change, so I ask.

Option B · an IMAP engine on the VPS

Your picture was right: the engine reads Alex’s sent mail and puts the conversation back into their inbox. IMAP is what makes the reading possible, and the price is Alex’s app password sitting on a rented server.

What it adds

Replies noticed by itself

  • No “they replied” click, and zero-touch is possible: FlyBy could watch everything he sends.

What it costs

Trust and upkeep

  • An app password per person, encrypted, on a host open to the internet.
  • An IMAP service to build and keep running; Yahoo and AOL only.

When it is worth it

After option A

  • If Alex and Sam find the “they replied” link annoying, B is the upgrade.

Which one fits, and what about Apple Mail?

Option C

Apple Mail extension

  • A FlyBy button in Mail’s Compose window on a Mac; it could add the Bcc address for you.
  • To verify: an extension only runs when Mail calls it, so waiting for a deadline needs a helper app that stays running on the Mac.

Why not first

Alex sends from his iPhone

  • His mails say “Sent from my iPhone”, and the iPhone has no Mail extensions.
  • Installing for other people needs a paid Apple developer account. On your own Mac a free one should be enough to test.

Outlook

A different door

  • Microsoft wants a sign-in app, not an IMAP password. Only worth it if Sam turns out to use it.

The steps from here

You

Two things to decide

  • Ask Sam (and Alex): which mail app, on which device?
  • Are you fine with one shared FlyBy mailbox for the three of you?

Me

Option A, small and testable

  • A new Bcc address form, a reminder email with links, and a sender allowlist.
  • Tested first with a spare address of yours; nothing goes to the VPS.

Later

B or C, only if A falls short

  • Decided after Alex and Sam have used A for a while.
